Most flash-card apps hand you somebody else's deck. That's fine until you need *your*
vocabulary: the words from this week's class, the phrases for a trip next month, the kanji
that keep tripping you up. Then you're back to typing cards by hand.

Calypr works the other way round. You say what you want to practise, and an agent writes the
cards, a few at a time, and keeps going for as long as you do. It notices what you miss and
gives you harder ones when you're ready. You don't build a deck; you have a drill.

This post shows the whole loop: starting in one click, how a drill works, making it yours,
hearing the words out loud, and practising with friends.

## Start in one click

[Sign up for free](/sign-up) with Google or GitHub. No card, no API key. Your dashboard opens
with a few ready-made starters, and **Language flash cards** is the first one.

Click it. Calypr creates the project and opens the chat, with a few things to try:

- *Spanish greetings*
- *Japanese hiragana: the first ten*
- *French food*

Pick one, or type your own topic, and press **Send**. A few seconds later your first cards
arrive.

## How a drill works

The agent sends **one to three cards at a time**, not a whole deck. Each shows the prompt
side: a word, a character, a phrase.

1. **Tap the card** to reveal the answer.
2. Mark it **Got it** or **Missed it**. Be honest: that's what it learns from.
3. The strip above the chat keeps score: how many you've got right, and how far through the
   cards on screen you are (*"1 correct · 1 of 3 answered"*).

Under the chat there are three shortcuts:

- **Next** for another batch on the same topic;
- **Harder** when the last batch was too easy;
- **Explain that** when you want the grammar or the story behind an answer.

You can also just type, as you would to a tutor: *"more verbs"*, *"only the ones I missed"*,
*"mix in some past tense"*. The agent remembers the conversation, so it adjusts to what
you've been getting wrong.

## Make the deck yours

The template is a starting point. Its behaviour lives in one place: the **System prompt** of
its **Agent** block. Close the chat panel, click the Agent block on the canvas, and add a line
or two to the end of the prompt. A few that work well:

- *"Use JLPT N5 vocabulary only. Put the kanji on the front, and the reading and meaning on
  the back."*
- *"For Mandarin: Simplified characters on the front; pinyin with tone marks and the English
  meaning on the back."*
- *"I'm a beginner in Spanish. Use everyday words, and always include the article (el/la)."*
- *"Only drill the vocabulary from this list:"* followed by your class word list.

Press **Save**, open the chat again, and your next drill follows the new rules.

## Hear it, too

Reading a word and saying it are different skills. The **Translate & speak** template takes
English, translates it into Mandarin, shows the transcript, and **speaks it aloud** with a
native-sounding voice, so you can listen and repeat.

It's two blocks you can change. To practise Japanese instead, edit the translator's System
prompt to translate into Japanese, and change the Voice block's instructions to Japanese
pronunciation. You'll find it under **Workflows** in the sidebar.

## Practise with friends

Click **Share** on any project to get a link. Anyone with it can run your drill in their
browser, **with no account**, and keep their own score. It's a nice way to give a study
group the same deck. Runs from the link use your credits, and each link is capped (25 runs by
default) so a link that travels further than you meant can't run away with them. You can
revoke it at any time.
